4. CAVEATS THAT LIMIT THE V2 PASS
---------------------------------
a) The gain is small against the run's own noise yardstick: -0.178 is 0.19 of coral's band (0.918). Only the 2017
   part (-0.091, 0.10 band) is exact; the 2019 part is POINT-MOVED.
b) The gain comes from ONE problem (coral), which has about 33 survey groups. The other four never moved.
c) The classifiers mostly separate periods by calendar-like signals, not by "conditions that look like the
   target". Global climate indices (pdo, tna, tsa, amo, nino, ao, nao) take one value per month or year for every
   cell, so AUC is 1.000 on pm25 V1 and V2, on fire V1 and V2, and on defo V1 and V2. On coral V2 2017, AUC is 1.000
   on ENSO lags (nino34a_lag9, nino34a_lag6) around the 2016 El Nino. That AUC is why the pm25 V1 and V2 2021/22
   ESS gates failed.
d) Feature-set deviations, logged per problem. pm25, fire, defo and coral used the engine's rule (the
   'conditional' columns are left out, treeroute/io/apply.py:57-58), which reproduces the plan.json counts
   116/125/303/63. The protocol's literal "verdict is not ban" wording would have given 140/132/304/66. ndvi used
   the protocol's fixed fallback subset (9 context + 20 tree features = 29), because 385 of its 492 tree features
   only exist inside an engine derive (datasets/ndvi/dataset.yaml:19-23). Its classifier was weak
   (AUC 0.52-0.58). defo kept the h_* columns (built from earlier labels), because the protocol's exclusion list
   names only hist_*. None of them is in any top-10.
e) The cap-then-renormalise step leaves some maximum weights above 20 (fire 20.74, coral up to 36.1). This follows
   the protocol (PROTOCOL.txt:84-85).

5. PLAIN ANSWER FOR THE OWNER
-----------------------------
Making the tuning period look like the test period changed the tool choice on only one of five problems, coral.
On pm25, ndvi, fire and defo every version kept the original tool, so it neither helped nor hurt there (fire's
penalty setting moved, but we can only score it at the saved setting). On coral, the cheating upper bound
(weighting toward the real test inputs) cut MAE from 6.698 to 6.359, so the idea has some real room. The honest,
deployable rolling version (V2), which only uses inputs from test years that have already happened, cut coral
MAE to 6.520, hurt nothing elsewhere, beat its shuffled-weights placebo, and passed all four pre-registered
success items. The other honest version (recency, V3) made coral slightly worse (6.736) and failed. So yes, there
is an honest version that helped without hurting, but the gain is small: about a fifth of coral's noise band,
half of it approximate, from one problem with about 33 survey groups. The classifier mostly tells the years
apart by the ENSO and climate-index "calendar" rather than by real look-alike conditions. Treat V2 as a lead to
pre-register and test in the next engine version, not as a proven improvement.

==== SKEPTIC ADDENDUM (2026-10-21; supersedes the "V2 passes" verdict above) ====
- Coral cross-fitting was by row, not by reef site; 419 sites appear in both sets. Re-run with site folds: V2 6.616 (-0.08, 0.09 of the band). The 2017 gain vanishes; all of what remains comes from 2019. (skeptic_check/coral_sitefold)
- With the protocol's literal feature list (66 columns), V2 fails the ESS gate in 2 of 3 years and even V1 falls back. (skeptic_check/coral_literal66)
- The weights mostly favour low-bleaching rows (weighted surface mean y 2.6-6.4 vs 9.5 unweighted), which suits the log-link tools and happened to fit the quiet years 2017-2019. They do not find look-alike conditions. The classifiers mostly separate periods by the climate-index calendar.
- On the other problems the honest version "did nothing" rather than "did no harm": it fell back or kept the pick. Fire's point moves also appear in the placebo, so they are noise.
- Defo's h_* columns are built from labels, so defo V1/V2 are not label-free (no pick changed).
- BOTTOM LINE: no robust, honest improvement. V2 is at most a weak lead. If tested in the next engine version, pre-register it with site-level folds, a frozen feature list and a low-bleaching-tilt check.
