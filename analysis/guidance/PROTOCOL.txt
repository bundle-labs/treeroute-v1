0. THE QUESTION, IN PLAIN WORDS
-------------------------------
The engine picks one tool by its MAE on past rows (the decision surface). On coral that pick was poor on test
(6.698 vs 6.147 for its unweighted twin; Part 601). Can we use what we KNOW about the test period without its answers
(its inputs, and the tools' own forecasts, which are built from inputs) to guide the choice better, on all five
problems, without making the others worse? Six fixed methods are tried. Test LABELS are never read by any method;
they are opened only at the very end to score the methods.

Known bias, stated now: M1-M3 were suggested by the coral failure itself (Part 603, PENDING_PARTS.md:510-526), so their
coral result is CIRCULAR. M4-M6 were not tuned on coral either, but this protocol was written after the coral numbers
of the chosen arm and twin were known. Any pass is a lead to pre-register on new data, never proof.

3. GATES (fail = that problem stops and is reported 'not run')
--------------------------------------------------------------
G1 Surface reproduction: the chosen arm's surface MAE at the adopted point (mean |y - P| over all inner_preds rows,
   decide.py:155) equals inner_candidates.csv for that arm and point within 1e-9 relative; every eligible arm's surface
   MAE at its own picked point equals inner_candidates.csv within 1e-9 relative; the eligible set recomputed by the N2
   rule from inner_candidates.csv equals adoption.json "eligible".
G2 Test reproduction: the chosen arm's pooled test MAE from test_preds equals verdict.json pooled_mae_adopted within
   1e-9 relative. SCORED ROWS = test rows with finite y and finite chosen prediction (the verdict's rows).
G3 Feature join (M6 only): 100% of surface and test rows find their feature row, else M6 is 'not run' for that problem.
G4 Two stages. STAGE A builds every method's test predictions and writes method_preds_<pkg>.parquet (unit, time,
   one column per method and placebo) plus stageA_<pkg>.json (alarm ratios, weights, chooser diagnostics, fallbacks),
   and the SHA-256 of all of them in stageA.sha256.txt, BEFORE stage B. Stage A reads test_preds with the key columns
   and the 'arm__' columns only, never 'y'. Stage B opens test y only to score.
Row-level predictions of every method are kept (method_preds_<pkg>.parquet), not only scores.

4. THE SIX METHODS (all fixed; nothing is tuned)
------------------------------------------------
Notation: C = chosen arm, T = twin, both at the adopted point. gap(rows) = mean |C - T| over the rows.
On the surface: all inner_preds rows. On test: all test rows with finite C and T (labels not consulted).

M1 ALARM->SIMPLER. ratio = gap(test) / gap(surface). The alarm FIRES iff ratio > 2.0 (strict).
   If it fires AND T is SIMPLER (section 2): every test row uses T. Otherwise every test row uses C.
   (So on pm25 and ndvi M1 equals the original pick whatever the ratio; on defo T is the plain twin.)
M2 ALARM->BLEND. Same ratio and threshold. If it fires: every test row uses 0.5*C + 0.5*T. Otherwise C.
M3 BLEND-2. Always 0.5*C + 0.5*T on every test row.
M4 BLEND-ELIGIBLE. Equal-weight mean of all ELIGIBLE arms (section 1 list), each at the ADOPTED point (no test column
   exists at any arm's own picked point; section 1 FIXED LIMIT). fire = mean of its 2 eligible arms; pm25 8; ndvi 4;
   defo 6; coral 12. The surface MAE of the same blend is reported beside (not used to decide anything).
M5 LEARNED BLEND (stacking). Weights w over the eligible arms, w >= 0, sum w = 1, minimising
   sum_i |y_i - sum_a w_a P_a,i| over surface rows, solved EXACTLY as a linear program:
   scipy.optimize.linprog(method='highs', default options), variables (w, t), minimise sum t_i subject to
   t_i >= y_i - P_i.w, t_i >= P_i.w - y_i, w >= 0, sum w = 1. After solving, w_a < 1e-9 is set to 0 and w renormalised.
   Row cap (no seeds): if a training set has more than 50,000 rows, keep the 50,000 with the lowest
   int(sha1(unit + '|' + str(time)).hexdigest(), 16).
   Group-blocked cross-fitting, K = 5: fold(row) = int(sha1(G).hexdigest(), 16) mod 5, where G = the row's 'group'
   (grid problems: pm25 127, ndvi 133, fire 40, defo 42 groups) and, on coral, G = the reef SITE = the part of 'unit'
   before '|' (1,421 surface sites). Fold-k weights are fitted on rows NOT in fold k and predict fold k; the
   cross-fitted surface MAE (CF-MAE) is the MAE of those out-of-fold predictions over all surface rows.
   Test weights = one fit on ALL surface rows (same cap). FIXED GATE: M5 is applied on test only if CF-MAE is
   strictly below the chosen arm's surface MAE at the adopted point; else every test row uses C (FALLBACK:CF).
   Reported beside, not used in the success rule: the ungated M5 (M5raw), fold weights, full-surface weights.
   If only one eligible arm existed M5 = that arm (does not occur: fire has 2).
M6 PER-ROW CHOOSER (hard) and M6s SOFT.
   Target per surface row: z = 1 if |y - T| < |y - C|, z = 0 if |y - C| < |y - T|; exact ties (equal absolute
   error) are left out of training and of the accuracy. Model (fixed): sklearn LogisticRegression(penalty='l2', C=1.0,
   solver='lbfgs', max_iter=2000, class_weight=None, fit_intercept=True). Nothing is tuned.
   Preprocessing (fit on the training rows only): every column numeric; drop a column > 50% missing or constant on the
   training rows; impute gaps with the training median; standardise with the training mean and sd; clip to [-10, 10].
   Same row cap (50,000 by the sha1 order) and the same K = 5 group/site folds as M5.
   Cross-fitted accuracy ACC = share of out-of-fold non-tie surface rows where (p > 0.5) == z; its base rate
   (share of z = 1) and its AUC are reported beside.
   FALLBACK (fixed): if the non-tie surface rows are fewer than 200, or either class has fewer than 20 rows, or
   ACC < 0.55, every test row uses C (FALLBACK:ROWS or FALLBACK:ACC), for both M6 and M6s.
   Otherwise one model is fitted on all non-tie surface rows (cap) and gives p = P(twin wins) per test row:
   M6  hard: test row uses T if p > 0.5, else C (p = 0.5 exactly -> C).
   M6s soft: test row uses (1 - p)*C + p*T.
   FEATURES (read-only, from D:\treeroute\analysis\adv_validation; no arm predictions are added as features):
     pm25  pm25/features_surface.parquet (join unit,time) and features_test.parquet (join unit, issue = time);

     ndvi  ndvi/features_ndvi.parquet (join unit, step = time; test on unit, issue): the fixed fallback subset,

5. PLACEBOS (fixed, no seeds; for the success rule they enter UNGATED)
----------------------------------------------------------------------
Fixed permutation pi of surface rows: rows sorted by int(sha1(unit + '|' + str(time))) ascending give order a;
rows sorted by int(sha1('placebo|' + unit + '|' + str(time))) ascending give order b; the row a[k] is paired with
the row b[k].
M5 placebo: y_placebo[a[k]] = y[b[k]] (labels shuffled across surface rows, predictions untouched). Fit the
   full-surface weights on y_placebo, apply to test, score on the true test y. Reported: ungated (used by the rule)
   and gated with its own CF-MAE on y_placebo.
M6 / M6s placebo: the feature row of b[k] is given to row a[k] (features shuffled against z; z untouched). Train,
   apply to the true test features, score. Reported: ungated (used by the rule; the model still predicts) and gated.
   This shows whether a gain comes from the features or only from the base rate of 'twin wins'.
M1-M4 have no placebo in this protocol (none was asked for); for them the placebo item reads 'N/A' and the
   circularity note of section 0 applies to M1-M3.

6. OUTCOME MEASURES (stage B)
-----------------------------
Per method x problem, on SCORED ROWS (section 3 G2):
  - test MAE; delta = method MAE - original MAE (negative = better); delta / band.
  - HELPS (delta < 0), SAME (method prediction equals C on every scored row, e.g. no alarm or a fallback),
    HURTS (delta > 0); a tally across the 5 problems, plus how many HURTS exceed 0.25 band.
  - per test year MAE beside (not used by the rule).
  - coral extra: MAE on BLEACHED-only rows (y > 0) and on SEVERE-only rows (y > 50; the bin used in
    analysis/coral_choice/drift/scripts/analyse_drift.py:107 and extra.py:30), with row counts and the original's
    MAE on the same rows; the per-programme MAE beside (evaluation confound, reported not used).
  - diagnostics: alarm ratio per problem (also per test year, beside); M5 weights and CF-MAE; M6 ACC, base rate,
    AUC, share of test rows switched to T, mean p.
  - Point flag: every arm used at the adopted point where its own pick differed is listed.
  - Sanity: on every problem the Part 603 numbers are recomputed (alarm ratio; M3 MAE pm25 3.584, ndvi 0.0314608,
    fire 0.004409, coral 6.394; M1 coral 6.147). A mismatch beyond rounding is reported and explained, not hidden.

7. SUCCESS RULE (fixed; judged per method; M6 and M6s judged separately)
-----------------------------------------------------------------------
A method is RECOMMENDED for a pre-registered test in the next engine version only if ALL hold:
  S1 coral improves: coral delta < 0 (test MAE below 6.69798924506233). Bleached-only and severe-only coral MAE are
     reported beside; they do not gate.
  S2 no harm: on pm25, ndvi, fire and defo, delta <= 0.25 x that run's band (pm25 0.0115530, ndvi 0.000149368,
     fire 3.39174e-06, defo 2.76124e-05; exact values from verdict.json at run time).
  S3 not a placebo effect: M5 -> the ungated M5 placebo's coral delta is strictly greater (worse) than M5's coral
     delta; M6 / M6s -> the ungated shuffled-feature placebo's coral delta is strictly greater than the method's.
     M1-M4: N/A (section 5).
  S4 not a no-op on coral: the method's coral predictions differ from C on at least one scored row (M5 and M6 did
     not fall back on coral).
If any fails: NOT RECOMMENDED, naming the failed item(s). M1-M3 passes are labelled CIRCULAR-ON-CORAL. Every pass is
a lead, not proof: it rests on one problem's flip and ~33-37 coral survey groups.
