5. ANSWER FOR THE OWNER (plain words)
-------------------------------------
Of the seven ways to second-guess the engine's pick, only the two alarm methods (M1 switch to the simpler twin,
M2 switch to a 50/50 blend) pass the pre-set rule; the other five each break something: always blending (M3) and
averaging all arms (M4) hurt other problems by 1 to 7 bands, the learned blend (M5) never moved off the pick, and
the per-row chooser (M6/M6s) hurt fire and did worse than its own shuffled-feature placebo on coral, so its coral
gain is not coming from the features. The best candidate for the next engine version is M1, the disagreement
alarm, with M2 as the cautious version that takes about half the coral gain (-0.33 vs -0.60 band) for half the
risk if the alarm ever fires wrongly. Be honest about what this shows: the alarm was invented after looking at the
coral failure, so its coral win is circular and does not count as new evidence. It passed "no harm" on the other
four problems only because it never fired there (and on pm25 and ndvi it could not have switched at all), so we
still do not know what it does when it fires on a problem where the pick was right. Even on coral the gain is one
bad year (2016) and one survey programme (McClanahan, 189 rows); in the other three years and on Reef_Check it is
slightly worse, and the whole thing rests on roughly 33-37 reef survey groups. So the right next step is to build
M1 (and M2) into the next engine as a pre-registered option and judge it on new problems or new years, not to
claim it as a result now.
