**Figure 5.** Skill by lead and by test year. **(a)** Pooled test MAE at each lead ("Now": the nowcast), in target units, with each panel on its own scale; burned fraction and forest loss are in units of 10⁻³ (4.5 means 0.0045). The final answers, the rejected pinned arms and the references at kept leads are as in Table 5. Filled circles, joined by a solid line, are the final answer where the skill guard passed. Open circles on a shaded column are declined leads, where the final answer is the decline target. Crosses, drawn to the right of their lead, are the pinned arm that the skill guard rejected. The dashed line is the decline target, the arithmetic normal (on coral bleaching the training median). The dotted line with small diamonds is the reference at kept leads where it differs from the decline target: the geometric normal on PM2.5 and forest loss, and the arcsine-scale normal on burned fraction; on NDVI and coral bleaching the reference is the decline target itself. Open diamonds show, at a declined lead, the pinned arm's reference, which is the normal in its link (geometric on PM2.5 at 12 months, arcsine-scale on burned fraction at 6 and 12 months) and which lies below the decline target. Passing the skill guard is not a verdict (Section 4.2). Triangles, drawn to the left of their lead, are damped persistence at the forecast leads of the gridded problems, scored on the test rows that have an earlier label (98.7–100% of them). Squares are predict-zero on the three zero-heavy problems; on PM2.5 and NDVI it is off the scale (17.52 and 0.4365). Persistence is not drawn for coral bleaching (Table 5). **(b)** The MAE reduction of the final answer against its reference, 100 × (reference − final answer) / reference, by test year, at the eight kept problem-leads; values above 0 favour the final answer, as in Figure 4 and Table 5. Circles are the nowcast, and down-triangles on a dashed line are the kept forecast lead. Grey squares are the same reduction for predict-zero on the zero-heavy problems; they are drawn once per problem because the value is identical at both kept leads. Each panel has its own y-axis: the yearly gains of burned fraction are at most 1.6%, and the yearly values of coral bleaching rest on 431–873 test survey visits. On PM2.5 at 6 months in 2022 the final answer is worse than its reference by 0.01% (Section 4.3 gives every per-year loss). All values are point estimates, because the source tables give no intervals for pooled or per-year MAE; Table 5 gives the contrasts over regions with jackknife intervals. Forest-loss data: Hansen et al. (2013).
