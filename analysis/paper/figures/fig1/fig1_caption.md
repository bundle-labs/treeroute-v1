**Figure 2. treeroute v1, from dataset package to forecast ladder.** The figure shows the design only, with no results. The only input set by hand is the dataset package (data, column facts, ban list, split file and an optional feature script that builds the router context). From it and from fixed engine constants, 36 written rules set every modelling setting in the parts below, before any compute (Supplement S7.10). Four disclosed qualifiers apply (Section 2.9; Supplement S1). The router context is made by the dataset, and NDVI's five context drivers are test-exposed. Coral bleaching enters as a table of survey visits whose feature timing and train/test marks the author vouches for; it has 46 training-only regions; and its 2016-2019 test window was set by hand after the yearly survey counts had been seen. Plug-and-predict is therefore claimed only for the four gridded problems. Colours: blue for the specialist trees; green for the routers; orange for the adaptation layer, the skill guards and the pinned arm; grey for the dataset package and settings, the unpenalised design columns (intercept and normal), the trees-off fit and the decline target. A white design block is the router context, and a white box with a padlock is a lock. Masked cells are hatched. MAE: mean absolute error. <!-- M4, M5 --> <!-- M6, K12, P22, P23 -->

**(1) Tree bank.** In each of the $G$ regions, 100 specialist trees (depth 8) are trained on bootstrap bags of the region's own training rows. Tree $k$ produces a tree answer $T_k$. Its corrector $C_k$ is a depth-6 tree fitted to the out-of-bag residuals, and it produces the corrected answer $T_k + C_k$. A tree with fewer than 50 out-of-bag rows has no corrector, so its corrected answer is $T_k$; this is the case for most trees on coral bleaching. The placebo of a corrector is a control and can never be selected. The tree inputs are the columns that the ban list keeps, lagged, together with the own-history features (none on coral bleaching). The router context is never a tree input. The tree-answer table holds every tree answer; its rows are location-steps (survey visits on coral bleaching) and its columns are the trees, grouped by region. For each location $u$, the trees of every region in its overlap block $\mathcal B(u)$ are masked. Its router may therefore use only the trees $\mathcal K(u)$ outside the block, and it sees only out-of-region predictions. Row $u$ illustrates a two-region block (its own region 2 and the overlapping region 3). Rows $u'$ and $u''$, in regions 1 and $G$, illustrate the common case, since most overlap blocks hold a single region. This leave-one-block-out tree bank is not a test on unseen regions; the test is a temporal hold-out (Figure 3). <!-- M11, M15, M40, K10, N8 --> <!-- M13, M35, K9 --> <!-- M16, M16a, M17, M18, K1 -->

**(2) Router.** Each problem gets one router type, set by its history depth $h$ (Table 1). The depth is the median number of valid labels per location (per reef site on coral bleaching) up to the end of the inner fit window. The per-location router ($h \ge 24$) serves PM2.5, NDVI and burned fraction, the region router ($2 \le h < 24$) serves forest loss, and the neighbour router ($h < 2$) serves coral bleaching. The per-location router fits one ridge for each location, to the linked label $\phi(y)$, on the location's own router fit rows. Its design has four parts: an intercept; the seasonal normal $n_u$ (the mean of $\phi(y)$ over the router fit rows in the same season), centred on its mean $\bar n_u$, as the anchor (unpenalised, its weight fitted); the router context $\mathbf z$; and the masked tree answers $\mathbf T$. The four parts are penalised by $0$, $0$, $a_{\text{ctx}}\kappa_{\text{ctx}}$ and $a_{\text{tree}}\kappa_{\text{tree}}$, where $a_{\text{ctx}}$ and $a_{\text{tree}}$ are penalty-grid levels and the penalty scale $\kappa$ is the mean diagonal of the block's weighted Gram matrix. A scored location with fewer than 24 router fit rows would use the region router instead; this short-history fallback routed no scored row in the runs (Supplement S7.10). The region router is one ridge pooled over each region, fitted on at most 60,000 router fit rows of whole bank locations, with no router context and no error weighting. It centres the normal on its mean $\bar n$ over these rows and penalises the trees by $\lambda\kappa^{c}_{\text{tree}}$ ($\lambda$: its penalty level; $\kappa^{c}_{\text{tree}}$: the penalty scale of the column-centred tree block). In both of these routers the intercept and the normal are unpenalised. Large penalties therefore shrink the fit toward the normal, and the trees-off fit equals the seasonal normal exactly (dashed arrows; asserted at every fit). This climatology identity thus holds on all four gridded problems. The neighbour router fits one ridge for each scored survey visit (the star in the inset) on a pool: among the 150 nearest earlier training visits (inside the dashed circle), the 50 that are most similar in tree inputs (dark dots; open rings: other training visits). It masks the visit's own overlap block and every block in the pool, and it uses the per-location penalty grid on $[\,1 \mid \mathbf z \mid \mathbf T\,]$, with only the intercept unpenalised. It has no normal and therefore no climatology identity. Its reference and its decline target are the training median, which is also its forecast when fewer than 12 visits are eligible. <!-- M21, M59, K6, K1 --> <!-- M22, M24, M32, K15, K16 --> <!-- M29, K18, conflict C18 (PAPER_FACTS section 12; its reading confirmed by the owner 2026-10-28, ruling AO3); anchor wording: ruling AN1, M22 --> <!-- M24, M27, K7 --> <!-- M23, M33, K8, M59 --> <!-- M28, M43, M44, K19, K20 -->

**(3) Adaptation layer (nowcast, $L = 0$).** An arm is the combination of three choices. The first is a link $\phi$: identity, $\log(y + c)$ or $\arcsin\sqrt{y/U}$, admitted by rules on the target's declared bounds and training values ($c$: a small positive log constant; $U$: the declared upper bound). The second is an answer type, plain or corrected. The third is a weighting: uniform, or error-weighted with $\omega = \hat\sigma^{-2}$, $\hat\sigma$ being the error-weight forest's prediction of the trees' error on the row (uniform only on the region router). Each arm takes the point of its router's penalty grid that has the lowest MAE (in units of $y$, after back-transformation and clipping) on the decision surface. The decision surface is the scored locations' rows in the inner score window, before the test window (on coral bleaching, that window's training visits, in one fold per region), as predicted by trees and routers fitted at the inner cut. The log constant $c$ and the error-weight forest's target come from the training cut $\tau$ and so use labels of the inner score window; for that reason the surface scores of the error-weighted arms are not fully out of sample. The skill guard admits an arm only if its surface MAE is strictly below (with no margin) that of its reference, which is its own link's trees-off fit (the training median on coral bleaching). The guard reuses the decision surface, and there is no separate hold-out. The admitted arm with the lowest surface MAE is adopted. If none is admitted, the layer declines to the decline target: the arithmetic normal, or the training median on coral bleaching. Trees-off fits and placebos can never be selected. On the gridded problems the reference of a log or arcsine arm is that link's back-transformed normal, which differs from the decline target. The adoption is locked before the test labels are opened for scoring. In the test round, the trees and routers are refitted at the training cut $\tau$, the test labels are opened, and the nowcast is forecast with the adopted arm, or with the decline target after a decline. <!-- M39, K27, M40, M41, M27, K17 --> <!-- M24, M42, M46, M19, K22, K30, K31 --> <!-- K22, K27, K28, M20 --> <!-- M40, M43, M44, M46, K33 --> <!-- M45, K33 --> <!-- M38, P17, K22, K37 -->

**(4) Forecast ladder.** Each lead $L \ge 1$ has a direct model of its own; $L_1 < L_2$ are the forecast leads of a problem (6 and 12 months for PM2.5, NDVI and burned fraction, 183 and 365 days for coral bleaching; forest loss has one, 1 year). At each lead, the tree bank is refitted at the fit cut $\tau_L$, and the arm and penalty adopted at the nowcast are pinned (its best tree arm and that arm's penalty if the nowcast declined). Every lead thus pins the nowcast's adoption directly. The pinned arm has to pass its own skill guard on the guard rows, with trees fitted at the guard cut $\tau^{g}_{L}$; if it does not, the lead declines to the decline target. The adoption of each lead is locked before its test labels are opened. Several of these settings are test-exposed, having been fixed after the test-window results of earlier project pipelines had been seen (Supplement S1): the penalty-grid centres, the region router's 60,000-row budget and the refit of the trees at the inner cut. <!-- M9 --> <!-- M19, M47, K21, K23, P17 --> <!-- M19, M24, P23, K22 -->
