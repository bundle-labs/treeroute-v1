**Figure 4.** The nowcast contrast per region, MAE(reference) − MAE(final answer), in target units; positive values favour the final answer. Each point stands for one scored region, and both MAEs are taken over that region's labelled test rows in the test window (Section 3.1). The reference is the one given in Table 4a. The panels are grouped by router type. Regions are sorted by contrast and are not named; the name and contrast of each region are in the archive's per-region tables (Software and data availability). Filled points are regions where the final answer is better, and open points are regions where it is worse. Grey squares are the four burned-fraction regions in which both MAEs are 0 (no burned area in the test rows, both forecasts 0); they are counted in the mean as 0. Three further burned-fraction regions without burned area count as better, because there the final answer forecast less burned area than its reference. Single regions carry no interval and are not tested on their own; the counts by sign are in Table 5. Below each panel are the equal-weight mean over regions (diamond) with its 95% jackknife interval (bar), and the equivalence band ±b (shaded), which is set from the run's own test-window arm contrasts. HELPS requires the bar to lie wholly above 0 and the diamond to lie at or above +b (Section 3.4; Table 5). The open triangle is the pooled MAE difference; it weights regions by their test rows and does not decide the verdict. On coral bleaching, the area of a marker shows the region's test survey visits (20 or fewer: smallest size). On burned fraction, the interval includes zero under the report-only blocked jackknife and under a post hoc t interval, and predict-zero and the geometric normal (post hoc) have a lower pooled MAE than the final answer (Sections 4.1 and 4.4). Forest-loss data: Hansen et al. (2013).
