**Figure 3.** Windows, cuts and locks of the five runs. **(a)** Each bar shows three windows: the inner fit window (light blue); the inner score window (dark blue), on which the adaptation layer makes its nowcast choices; and the test window (orange), which is a temporal hold-out in every region. The test window of coral bleaching was set by hand, by leaving out the 2020 survey visits (hatched). The grey line covers the years before the first fit year that are taken up by the longest driver lag the engine applies (there is none on coral bleaching, whose feature timing is set in its dataset package). The black vertical lines mark the inner cut $\tau_{\text{in}}$ and the training cut $\tau$, which are the last steps whose labels may be used by the objects fitted in the inner round and in the test round, respectively; on the gridded problems, the log-link constant and the error-weight target of the inner round come from the training cut. Under each bar, for one example lead $L$, are the fit cut $\tau_L$ (filled circle), the guard cut $\tau^{g}_L$ of the skill guard's trees (open circle) and the guard rows $(\tau_{\text{in}}, \tau_L]$ (dark line). On the guard rows the pinned arm must beat its reference, or the lead declines; the guard also reads the link constant of the fit cut and, for a weighted arm, that cut's error-weight target (cut rules: Section 2.6). At every lead of every run the adoption was locked and the label vault then opened that lead's test labels for scoring, in the same recorded second (padlock; its position marks the window it guards, not the date of the lock). Before a lock, test-window labels entered only as own-history features and persistence inputs, each as known at the issue step of its row (gridded problems; Supplement S7.6). The runs were locked one after another, and the other four were locked after PM2.5's test labels had been opened (Supplement S4). Diamonds mark test-exposed choices (Table 1), so the results are locked but not fully confirmatory.
