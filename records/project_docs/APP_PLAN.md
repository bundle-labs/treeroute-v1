## In short

- **One engine, one path, no problem names.** Every environmental problem goes through the same code: every group's own specialist trees, a router on each unit's own history (or on its group, or its neighbours) that never sees its own group's trees (nor those of groups sharing its cells), one Type-2 layer, one forecast ladder and one report. The engine has no per-problem adapters, presets or branches.
- **Plug and predict.** The ONLY hand-set input is a **dataset package**. It holds the data tables, the column facts, an optional feature script (which also makes the router context), the ban list, the exclusions and an optional Type-3 plug-in. There is no hand-set donor/test split any more: every group is trained and tested. Every modelling setting follows a written rule (Section 2). Each value is written to `derived.json` and printed by `treeroute plan`, with its rule and the numbers it read, before any compute is spent.
- **Leave-one-region-out (LORO; owner ruling, 2026-09-28 night).**
  - No group is held back as a "donor" or a "test" group. Every group gets its own specialist trees, trained on its rows up to the donor train end, and every group is tested.
  - A unit's router never sees its own group's trees. They are dropped as columns, and a guard asserts it. Some groups share cells: 8 pairs of regions overlap on the panel (brigalow_belt holds 8,251 of australian_mulga's cells). So groups that share or touch a unit location form one "overlap block", found by a rule on the static table, and a router drops the trees of its whole block (R14).
  - The time split, the router on the unit's own history and the issue-date cuts do not change.
  - Scored units are spread over all groups by one rule. With K = 20 (R18, the working value), the total is about the old one on PM2.5 (1,905 against 2,000), but fire's doubles (2,000 against 1,000), NDVI's falls to 0.45x (1,995 against 4,400) and defo's rises 1.22x (85,680 against 70,487). **One engine constant cannot give every package its old total, which ruling D asks for.** The owner chooses (9.1 item 6a): one K = 20, each package's old total, or one larger K.
  - The trees and the region router still learn from 100 units per group (4,285 on defo).
  - The old hand-split test groups get a free "continuity" row from the same run.
  - Rivals (4.6): own-history rivals need no retraining, the gauged global rival is trained once, and donor-only rivals are trained on 10 region folds (an overlap block stays in one fold).
- **Every door for a hand-set number is closed, not only the package.** None of these can change a result any more: command-line flags, environment variables, the machine file, the order of the group list, a hand-picked donor/test split (LORO), the size of a smoke run, or the free disk on the day. Threads, tree-prediction workers, router workers and the backend become engine constants. Unit counts come from the data only. Groups are a set, sorted by name, and each group's trees and correctors are seeded from its own name.
- **Not hard-coded for these five problems.** The engine knows units, groups, time steps and facts, never "PM2.5" or "fire". Monthly, annual and N-day grids (including 8- and 16-day composites that restart every January) and irregular events (single or repeat visits) all fall under one cadence rule. The five problems are acceptance tests kept inside their own packages. Six synthetic toy packages will prove that a sixth problem needs no engine edit: an 8-day step that restarts each January, seasonal gaps, small polygon groups, rare yes/no events, irregular repeat visits and a 7-year record. One more fixture must be refused (presence-only events). Under the fast track these toys, and the event code they test, are built in M8 (9.1 item 1). A test fails if a dataset name, a year, a drive letter or a unit name appears in engine code.
- **Built for adding problems later.** The package format already reserves what new problems will need: cell-to-polygon weights, neighbour tables, time and space aggregation, a report-only role for operational products, and a base-period fact for anomaly columns. A no-op rule keeps later growth cheap. An engine change does not force re-runs if three things hold for every existing package: its derived.json and smoke output stay byte-identical, AND either a one-group, full-width probe stays byte-identical or the changed functions are shown never to run on that package at full size.
- **The five problems (Section 3):** beyond what the owner already ruled, thirteen settings move against the shipped runs. Four are on fire and defo: fire's inner window, first year and unit picks, and defo's bank-unit count (now 4,285, with the test-pass router keeping evenly spaced whole cells). One is on coral (optional track). The rest are small and uniform: one more history column, two defo columns the Q34 review must rule on, and seeds. All are listed in plain words in 3.3. The LORO ruling also changes every package's test set (3.2 and "LORO in plain words" in 3.3). That change was ruled by the owner, so it is not counted among the thirteen.
- **Leak safety is tighter.** Cuts apply to every fitted object, the inner guard included, and event leads get their own cut, counted in days. A router never sees the trees of its own overlap block, and a Type-3 plug-in gets tree answers only through the same mask. The neighbour kind's leave-one-group-out surface gets its own bank, cut at the inner-fit end. Two in-sample effects that LORO brings are disclosed and put to the owner: the GLS weight model now sees the scored units' fit rows (9.1 item 6e), and the A7 decision-surface bias grows (9.1 item 4). Unsigned timing facts cannot enter the headline. Router context chosen from data must declare what data it saw.
- **Faster runs, same numbers (4.5).** Why a run takes so long: a nowcast spends 60% of its time asking about 10,700 trees for their answers. It asks for most answers twice, one tree at a time on one core. Another 25% goes on about 2,000 small router fits done one after another. Three engine changes fix that: tree answers are built once per row, the trees answer on 8 threads, and 8 router cells are solved at once. Speedups 1 and 2 must give byte-identical output against the current code. Speedup 3 must give byte-identical output for any number of router workers; it moves today's numbers by up to 3.6e-13 ug/m3 (measured, Part 573; every decision unchanged), so the goldens are captured under the new pin. ## 1. The dataset package: the only hand-set input

### 1.1 Folder

```
<package>/
  dataset.yaml              name, store paths, shared-catalog includes. Nothing else.
  catalog.yaml              problem-relative facts (roles, target facts); includes shared store catalogs by sha256
  ban.yaml                  one verdict per column (closed world)
  split.yaml                exclusions; optional seen_during_design; optional continuity_groups (reporting
                            only, if the owner keeps it here: 9.1 item 6g).
                            No donor or test list: every group is trained and tested (LORO)
  exceptions.yaml           signed provenance flags, disclosed defects and test-exposure disclosures (may be empty)
  expected_derived.json     this package's acceptance values (Section 3.2); tests only, never read by a run
  AUDIT_CHECKLIST.md        the human checks no code can do, signed
  ingest/                   the dataset's own code that made its tables (hashed; never run by the engine)
  features/build.py         optional: engineered columns, incl. router context
  type3/plugin.py           optional: a human-designed gate
  type3/shipped_constants.yaml
  tables/unit_map.parquet   optional, reserved: source unit -> unit, weight (polygons made from grid cells)
  tables/adjacency.parquet  optional, reserved: unit, neighbour, weight
  data/<table>/group=<g>/year=<yyyy>/part-0.parquet   default layout for NEW data; existing stores are read in place
```

The engine refuses any other top-level key or file role (G-NOKNOB).

### 1.4 Ban verdicts (ban.yaml)

- **keep**.
- **keep_unreviewed**: the verdict is not yet reviewed, but the timing facts ARE signed. Allowed only with no forward reach and no lineage flag, and listed in every report (A5).

### 1.5 Feature script contract (features/build.py, optional)

- The script gets a reader with sentinels already masked. It writes its outputs to the package's features store with a code fingerprint. Every output column is declared: derived_from, operation, a backward-only window, reads_target.

- The checker recomputes a stride sample of every engineered column from its declaration and refuses any mismatch (G-DERIVED).

## 2. Derived automatically

Each rule is a pure function in derive/. It writes its value, the inputs it read (with hashes) and its margin to any threshold into derived.json. A value within 20% of a threshold is flagged 'borderline'. That flag is reported, never a choice. All constants sit in engine_constants.py with their origin (3.1).

**Words under LORO (owner ruling, 2026-09-28 night).** Every group is a donor to every other group, and every group is tested. So in this section:
- **Donor rows** means the labelled rows of every group's BANK units at or before the cut (R18).
- **Test units** means the SCORED units of every group.
- **A group's own trees** are the 100 trees trained on its own bank units. They never enter its own routers, nor the routers of any group in its overlap block (R14).

The rules below read donor rows for c, links, tolerances, event rates and the GLS model exactly as before. Only the set of groups behind those rows has changed.

### 3.3 Flags in plain words

1. **Fire's inner window becomes 3 years (2016-18) instead of 2.** It follows PM2.5's rule. The router's inner fit shrinks from 180 to 156 rows per cell.
2. **Fire's first year becomes 2003.** Once annual columns are lagged (Q3 a), 2002 has no source year, exactly as for PM2.5. Fire keeps 192 rows per cell at the test cut instead of 204.

At the test pass the fit rows grow to 17 per cell, so the router keeps about 3,529 evenly spaced whole cells (59,993 rows). **New:** the test-pass router now strides whole cells. ## 4. The engine

### 4.1 What every run does (one pipeline for every package)

1. Load the package and fingerprint it (every file, the ingest code, the data manifest). Run the quick checks. Any BLOCK stops here.
2. Run derive/ to write derived.json. The run refuses if derived.json differs from the one `plan` showed.
3. Build the dense unit x step grid over every row (labels may be NaN); events keep one row per visit and an as-of label view. Mask sentinels at read. Join 1:1 with row-count asserts. Apply computed lags, bans and freezes. Attach the checked outputs of the feature script (as-of joins per issue date for events).
4. Build the own-history block (R17).
5. Derive the overlap blocks from the static table (R14). For every group (LORO), pick its bank units by stride and its scored units as an evenly spaced subset of them (R18). Hash both lists and the blocks.
6. Through FitContext, fit on donor rows (every group's bank units) at or before the cut: one bank holding every group's specialist trees, each trained on its own group's rows (R19); out-of-bag correctors; placebo correctors; the GLS weight model with its donor-row tree pass (R25); and, for the neighbour kind, one more bank cut at the inner-fit end for its leave-one-group-out surface (R13).
7. Build the tree-answer tables per group, in row blocks, over every scored row (and, for the region kind, every fit-unit row; in a per-unit package, also the rows at or before the cut of every bank unit of a group that has a fallback unit, R12, R20, which plan lists). Each row goes through all the trees once, over history and test rows, with the trees answering in parallel (4.5).
8. On the decision surface, score every link x arm x weighting x penalty point, plus trees-off, with router units solved in parallel (4.5). Every router drops its own overlap block's tree columns, and each fit asserts it (G-DISJOINT). Apply the N2 guard and MAE adoption. adoption.json is hashed before the test labels open.
9. Test pass: score every arm (all saved) from the tables of step 7, computing only rows not built yet (the placebo table is built once, here). Clip. Compute the baselines.
10. Type-3 plug-in, if present: 'shipped' and 'blind', after the hash, once per issue step. Its tree answers come only through the masked services (1.6).
11. Forecast ladder, per derived lead:
    - the lead's cut and the inner guard's cut;
    - lagtrain pinned to the nowcast adoption, with a weight refit per lead only if the pinned arm is weighted;
    - the guard, persistence baselines and the lead-0 contract;
    - at the longest lead, the pinned arm's placebo (Part 559, F45).

    The nowcast band decides at every lead (F57).
12. Report: metrics, the single verdict, the jackknife over all groups with the blocked jackknife beside, per-year / per-group (with the noise note) / per-stratum tables, the continuity row, the seen_during_design row, nearest-donor distances and scores by distance band, part_draft.md.

### 4.5 Runtime and speed

**Why a run takes so long, in one paragraph (the logged run, old split; LORO's numbers are in the estimate at the end of 4.5).** A nowcast asks about 10,700 trees (100 per donor group) for an answer on every row it scores. On PM2.5 that is about 400,000 rows for the inner decision and 500,000 for the test pass. Today it asks one tree at a time, on one of the machine's 16 threads, and it asks for most answers twice. That is 60% of the run. Another 25% is the router: about 2,000 small per-cell fits (20 test groups x 100 cells), done one after another. Reading the data (8%) and fitting the trees (6%) are the rest.

PM2.5's U2 nowcast, as logged (D:/pm25_adapt/u2/run.log, 437 minutes = 7.3 hours):

| Phase | Minutes | Share |
|---|---|---|
| Pool: stream 127 regions into one file | 35.5 | 8% |
| Bank: fit 10,700 trees, their correctors and placebo correctors | 26.3 | 6% |
| Tree-answer tables: every tree's answer for every row, written to disk | 264.4 | 60% |
| - inner plain / inner corrector (407,388 rows each) | 31.6 / 53.2 | |
| - test plain / test corrector / test placebo (503,244 rows each) | 33.4 / 68.4 / 77.8 | |
| Router fits and scoring (per-cell solves on pinned threads) | about 110 | 25% |

**Correction to the router line (LORO measurement, 2026-09-28 night).** U2 searched a ONE-point grid (its log: "PENALTY GRID: 1 levels x 1 ratios"), so its ~110 min understates the router. Part 556's full run searched the 35-point grid. It spent 75.7 min on 4 inner arms, 18.9 min per arm against U2's 5.2 (D:/pm25_adapt/full/run.log). At that rate PM2.5's router without GLS is about 72 min at 1.75x, not the 37-55 min this section first counted. The LORO estimate at the end of 4.5 uses the corrected rate.

In plain words: the run asks 10,700 experts (the trees) for an answer on every row, then fits a small router per cell. Three habits make it slow:
1. **The same answers are asked for twice.** The test tables hold 503,244 rows: the same 407,388 history rows the inner tables already answered, plus 95,856 new rows, from the same bank. About 85 of the 264 minutes recompute answers that already exist.
2. **One expert at a time, on one core.** The writer (pm25_adaptation.py:655-690; its forecast copy at pm25_forecast.py:233-262) loops over row blocks and, inside each block, asks one tree after another. The machine has 8 cores (16 threads), and no tree's answer depends on another tree.
3. **One router cell at a time.** Each cell's fit is small (about 200 fit rows), so 4 BLAS threads on one cell mostly wait on each other. No cell's fit depends on another cell's.

**Speedup 1: tree answers are built once per row, not once per pass.**
- Each table is kept on disk, keyed by bank fingerprint + table kind (plain, corrector, placebo) + the row key. The row key is unit + step for grids, and unit + event date + issue date for events, because event features depend on the issue date. A pass that needs a row already in the table reads it; only new rows are computed.
- The inner decision and the test pass share one plain table and one corrector table, each over history + test rows.
- The placebo table (never selectable) is built once, after the adoption hash, over history + test rows. It also serves any inner placebo rows the report shows.
- GLS-weighted arms read the same tables, because weights change the router, not the tree answers.
- Rows sit unit by unit in step order, as today (pm25_adaptation.py sorts by cell and month before any table is written), so every router gets the same numbers in the same order.
- **Disk cost:** the plain and corrector tables must stay on disk from the inner decision to the test pass, so two tables exist at once instead of one (PM2.5: about 43 GB of the about 53 GB free). plan's disk check decides which tables stay, the costlier corrector table first. A table that does not fit is rebuilt after the hash, per group, exactly as today. Like per-group batching, this changes hours, never a number (1.7). **Under LORO** a table holds every scored row of every group through all the bank's trees. That is 24.4 GB per kind on PM2.5 and 26.7 GB on NDVI, so one table stays between passes on each. On defo the region routers also read every fit-unit row, so a table is about 56 GB per kind. None can stay, and both kinds are rebuilt per group after the adoption hash; the estimate prices that.
- Effect on PM2.5: five tables become three, about 180 minutes on one core instead of 264.
- Forecasts already build one table per bank over every row of the test units and read predecessor rows from it, so speedup 1 changes nothing there.

**Speedup 2: the trees answer in parallel.**
- For each row block, the trees are split across 8 worker threads in one process. Each thread writes its own columns of the block buffer, and the block goes to disk as one slab, as today (row blocks, never column by column). Threads share the trees, so RAM does not grow (the block buffer stays about 0.5 GB).
- A tree's answer is fixed by the tree and the row, so the bytes are the same for any number of workers.
- The same writer serves the GLS donor-row pass (R25) and the forecast tables.
- The worker count (8) is an engine constant (C14), recorded in the manifest. The 'off' path (1 worker, tables rebuilt per pass) exists only inside tests, never as a flag.
- Two runs of a 10-second synthetic probe on this machine gave 3.2-3.9x at 4 threads, 5.0-6.6x at 8 and 6.2-7.9x at 16, byte-identical every time. The probe was bench_tree_threads.py (320 PM2.5-shaped trees, 100,000 rows, sklearn 1.6.1, Python 3.9.13), run on 2026-09-28. Both runs are logged in the M0 Part, and the plan cites both.

**Speedup 3: router cells are solved in parallel.**
- Per-unit and neighbour routers solve one small problem per unit. Today they run one unit after another with BLAS on 4 threads. Instead, 8 units are solved at once, each with BLAS pinned to 1 thread. Worker count and BLAS threads are engine constants (C14, A8).
- A unit solved with BLAS on 1 thread gives the same bytes whatever the number of workers. Against today's shipped thread setup it differs by up to 3.6e-13 ug/m3 on the real smoke (Part 573; all of it from the BLAS thread count, every decision unchanged), because BLAS adds numbers in a different order. So the goldens are captured under the new pin, as A8 already plans for the thread pin, and the gap to today's numbers is printed.
- bench_router_threads.py, on 2026-09-28, ran 240 synthetic PM2.5-shaped cells (204 fit + 48 test rows, 10,700 tree columns, the 35-point shared-Gram grid). It gave 2.8x, 3.6x and 3.9x at 4, 8 and 16 workers against today's setup. The output was identical for every worker count and 2e-15 from today's numbers on that synthetic bench (3.6e-13 on the real smoke, Part 573). The bench ran in RAM, while real rows come from the HDD memmap. The previous draft counted 2-3x; Part 573 measured 1.7-1.8x at smoke width, which the LORO estimate uses.
- The region router is one pooled solve per group (defo), so it keeps BLAS at 4 threads and is not sped up (owner ruling C: a 4-thread mode with a 1e-9 match tolerance against its golden). **Under LORO** defo has 42 pooled solves of 4,101 columns instead of 17 of 2,501, so the region router takes about 306 min instead of 45. The tree block and its trace normalisation do not change between the 3 links. So the tree block's Gram (T'T), almost all of the work, could be computed once per group and shared across the links. The per-unit link-matched normal column (U-1) does change with the link, so its border with the tree block is recomputed per link. bench_region_gram.py timed T'T alone, so the cut from 306 min to about 125 roughly holds. It is an optional boilerplate change, gated by byte identity; a Gram assembled in blocks may not be byte-identical to a one-shot Gram, and if it is not, the change is dropped (9.1 item 6d).

Under LORO a rival must face the same test as the engine: every group is scored, and nothing a rival uses for a group may have learned from that group in a way the engine's router cannot. Whether a rival must be retrained depends only on what it learns from.

| Rival kind | Examples (PM2.5 board letters, pm25_rivals.py header) | Under LORO | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Own history only** | N (recent-level and trend normals); persistence and damped persistence (R31); the per-unit MOS (board B) | **No retraining.** Each one is fitted on the scored unit's own rows at or before the cut, so it runs on every group's scored units as it is. | It never sees another unit, so which groups are donors cannot change it. MOS takes a rival column z from a donor-only rival. Its z comes from that group's fold model (below), so MOS carries the fold disclosure too. **Owner choice (9.1 item 6c):** the old Board B picked its rival trial jointly with the MOS by inner MAE, and under LORO a trial's z from the one tuning run is in-sample for about 3/4 of groups. |
| **Gauged global** | H (one LightGBM on every group up to the cut, own history included) | **Trained ONCE,** on every group's bank units' rows at or before the cut. Its tuning fit (rows up to the inner-fit end) barely changes on PM2.5: 2,133,600 rows, against the logged 2,127,178 (D:/pm25_rivals/full/rivals.log, H's tuning fit). Its fit at the cut is 127 x 100 x 204 = 2,590,800 rows. | It already sees the scored units' own history, the same information the router gets (owner ruling D). **Disclosed:** it also sees the other units of the scored unit's own group up to the cut, which the per-unit router does not. |
| **Donor-only** | R (land-use-regression ridge) and G (LightGBM); F is dropped with a deviation note (Q45) | **Trained on 10 region folds.** Groups go to folds round-robin over sorted overlap blocks (C17), so groups that share cells share a fold. | **Operational products** | M (MERRA-2, board C) | **Nothing to train.** Side rows only (Q46 a). | Not a learner. |

- **So: are rivals retrained for each test region? No.** Only the donor-only rivals are retrained, 10 times (one model per fold), not once per region. The own-history rivals are not retrained, and the gauged global rival is trained once.
- **Tuning (9.1 item 6c).** The old board picked hyperparameters on a fixed holdout: every 4th donor region in frozen order, fitted up to 2016 and scored on 2017-2019 rows (pm25_rivals.py:317-319). It then refitted the pick on all donors up to 2019. Proposed for LORO: the same rule over all overlap blocks. The holdout is every 4th block in sorted order, and the rule runs once. Then each of the 10 fold models refits the pick on its own groups. It reads no test-window label. **Disclosed:** the hyperparameters are tuned once on the pre-cut rows of groups that a fold model later "never saw", as the engine's own decision surface does. A quirk, harmless: holdout positions 0, 4, 8, ... taken mod 10 fall only in even folds.
- **Board B under LORO (owner choice, 9.1 item 6c).** (a) Board B takes Board A's pick and its z comes only from the fold models; this is a disclosed change from the old joint pick. (b) Every trial runs on all 10 folds, so every z stays out-of-fold for the joint pick: about 10x the tuning cost, roughly +15 h on PM2.5 alone. No default.
- **Pairing.** Rivals are re-paired on the engine's saved rows (F65): the same scored units and the same normal, with row order asserted.
- **Guard.** Each rival run's manifest holds the fold assignment and each fold model's groups. G-DISJOINT asserts that every donor-only prediction for a group, and every z row Board B reads, came from the fold model that did not see it.
- **Cost (rough, unmeasured).** PM2.5's board as logged (D:/pm25_rivals/full/rivals.log): cache 40.8 min, ridge R 37.2 min (12 trials), LightGBM G 55.5 min of trials + 3.7, gauged H 10.9 min, M 1.9 min, N 0.1 min. Under LORO, N and M do not change and H is fitted once (2,590,800 rows at the cut). R and G are tuned once (tuning fit about 1.6M rows up to the inner-fit end, against the logged 1.34M), then refitted on 10 folds at the cut of about 2.33M rows each (about 0.9 x 2,590,800; the 1.92M first given here was the count up to the inner-fit end). G's logged refit took 3.7 min and one R trial about 3 min, so the 10 fold refits add about 1-2 h on PM2.5. Board B option (b) would add about +15 h on PM2.5. NDVI (441 features) and defo (333) scale up, so all four boards might take 15-25 h. None of this is in the headline hours; the rival-board command is M8.

### 6.2 Adding a new problem, end to end

**What you provide (the only hand-set part):**
1. `py -3.9 -m treeroute new D:/datasets/<name>` writes an empty package: dataset.yaml, catalog.yaml, ban.yaml (every column UNDECLARED), split.yaml (empty), exceptions.yaml (empty), AUDIT_CHECKLIST.md, and a starter features/build.py (season-key sine/cosine and absolute latitude as router context). Under the fast track `new` comes in M8; until then, copy one of the four packages and empty it.
2. **A target ingest script** (in ingest/). It writes the target table (unit id in the fixed format, group id, time, y) as a complete census, with invalid labels as NaN, plus the static table. This is normally the only code a new problem needs.
3. **Store paths** in dataset.yaml. Include any shared store catalog (for example the 170-column monthly panel) by sha256.
4. **Facts** for the target and any new columns, via `treeroute catalog export/import`: the target's cited legal range and window, co-measured answers for every listed candidate, and calibration_through or computed_from where they apply.
5. **Optional feature work:** edit the starter script; add aggregations, a unit_map or an adjacency table if the problem needs them.
6. **A verdict per column** in ban.yaml, using the exported CSV (or catalog_review.html once it exists).
7. **Exclusions only** (optionally seen_during_design). There is no split to hand-set: every group that is not excluded is trained and tested (LORO). A continuity list is only for a package that had an earlier hand split, and only if the owner keeps that list in the package (9.1 item 6g).
8. **Optional Type-3 plug-in.**

**What the engine derives (no questions asked):** all of R1-R36. `treeroute plan` (and `inspect`, in M8) shows the router kind, windows, links, leads and unit counts as soon as the target table exists.

**What the engine refuses.** Each refusal prints a rule id, the columns, a plain-words reason, the fix and a Part number.
- any modelling key, unknown CLI flag, or code outside the two slots (G-NOKNOB);
- a column without facts or a verdict, or a listed column that is absent (G-CLOSED);
- a kept column with unsigned timing facts in a headline run (G-FACTS);
- a column with forward reach or an unknown window at lag 0 (G-LAG);
- own-lag, trailing-mean or cumulative columns of the target (G-HIST);
- a target without a cited legal range, with fewer or more than one target column, a constant target, a presence-only table, fewer than 3 groups, or a target calibrated past the donor end (G-SUPPORT, G-EPOCH);
- non-trailing or mismatching engineered columns, or a feature script that fits a model on the target (G-DERIVED);
- a co-product or a model trained on the target without a signed exception (G-LINEAGE);
- a donor or test list in split.yaml (G-NOKNOB), or a listed group that is not in the data (G-SPLIT);
- coverage too short even after the short-record ladder (R5): fewer than 6 years from Y0 to Y_end (3 test + 2 inner score + 1 inner fit), i.e. about 7 years of target data with the lag year. Years are calendar years, so a season crossing Dec/Jan is split;
- a Type-3 plug-in that reads test labels before the adoption hash or fits outside FitContext (G-LOCK, G-FITCUT);
- a plan whose disk or RAM does not fit (G-DISK);
- any write under a shipped path (G-NOOVERWRITE).

**Then:** `check --deep`, `plan`, `smoke` (plumbing only), `run` (prints the command), `report`, log the Part. `treeroute next` (M8) names the next missing step at any point.

### 6.3 Interface

1. **CLI (now).** Typer 0.23.2 on Python 3.9.13, both installed. Each step is one command that writes one file; `treeroute next` (M8 under the fast track) makes the CLI a guided wizard. Only the CLI runs jobs, and only as commands the owner pastes into his own terminal. That gives crash isolation and keeps RAM free on a 15.8 GB machine.
2. **Before submission:** the CSV round trip for the Q34 review and the timing-fact signing. catalog_review.html comes in M8 under the fast track, or in M1 in the full plan. Reports are self-contained HTML plus CSVs and part_draft.md.
3. **After submission (Dec-Jan):** a PySide6 6.10.3 QWizard with nine pages over the same functions. Quick checks run inside the GUI. Deep scans and runs start only as a copied command or a detached, visible console. The GUI never touches thread counts and imports no engine numbers, so it may run on the installed Python 3.12.10.

There is no web UI: Streamlit, Panel and Gradio need Python 3.10 or newer, and a browser competes for RAM. Notebooks are read-only examples.

## 10. Risks

- **Schedule.** About 18.5-22.5 agent days plus 9.25-11.25 owner days before the runs (fast track, LORO included), as floors. Runs from about Wed 28 Oct to Mon 9 Nov leave about 16 days for writing, if the speedups hold. One late bug re-runs every package already run, unless the no-op rule applies.
- **The speedups are only partly proven.** Part 573 measured speedup 2 at 8.1x on tree answers (with the finiteness scan done once per block) and speedup 3 at 1.7-1.8x, both at smoke width and byte-identical. The LORO estimate uses 8.1x and 1.75x (41.3 h), and 3x and 1.7x in its conservative case (53.8 h; re-priced 2026-10-05 for owner rulings I1 and H2, 4.5; was 42.5 / 54.8 h). An hour of full load on a laptop meets heat and HDD limits, and the full-width probe has not run. If a gate fails, that speedup is dropped and its hours return. Without the speedups the LORO design was not re-modelled: the old split needed 88-116 h of runs (110-165 h of machine budget with weighted refits and the buffer), LORO would need more, and A11 applies.
- **Speedup 3 moves numbers by rounding.** Pinning router BLAS to 1 thread changes today's numbers by up to 3.6e-13 ug/m3 (Part 573; every decision unchanged on the smoke). The goldens are captured under the new pin, so the replay gates compare like with like. A grid pick that ties within about 1e-12 could in principle flip; the printed gap shows whether any did.
- **Disk for build-once.** Under LORO a table is 24.4 GB per kind on PM2.5 and 26.7 GB on NDVI, so each keeps one table between passes and rebuilds the other. Defo's (about 56 GB per kind) cannot stay, so both kinds are rebuilt per group after the hash; the estimate prices that. A fuller D: costs hours, never a number. M0's backups stay small (47 MB) because run89full and the banks are protected by hash, not copied (F54).
- **GLS costs more than router work.** Its donor-row tree pass is about 4.4 times a LORO test table on PM2.5 (2,133,600 / 480,060 rows), per cut. If a nowcast adopts a weighted arm, every forecast lead refits its weights with another pass (+6.9-12.3 h, 4.5). Under LORO the weight model also sees the scored units' fit rows (R25); fitting it on folds would add about +10.6 h per cut (9.1 item 6e). More arms also mean more selection noise on small inner surfaces; the placebo per link x weighting and the band guard against false 'helps' claims.
- **Owner days are the critical path.** The Q34 review (about 250 new verdicts; Part 431 took 8 auditors for one problem) now comes with signing the timing facts of every kept column, by Tue 27 Oct, and under the fast track in a CSV rather than the review page. Owner days are the limit; if they slip, the run start slips.
- **No escape hatch, by design.** A dataset that truly needs a different setting cannot get one without an engine change, which re-runs every package. That is the price of uniformity, and the reason the rules are signed now. The reserved slots and the no-op rule lower the cost of a later addition.
- **The no-op rule could become a back door.** A smoke never reaches full-size paths: the region cap, the fallback router, the event floor, batching, the table schedule. That is why the rule also needs a one-group full-width probe, or proof that the changed code never runs on that package.
- **Rules can misfire on unseen data.** They were checked on five problems. Under the fast track the six toy packages, and the event code they test, come only in M8, so the event rules are signed but not run end to end before the freeze. A sixth real dataset may still hit a gap. Mitigation: derived.json shows margins and 'borderline' flags, and plan prints them before any compute.
- **Penalty grids depend on bank width.** The tree block's scale depends on the number of tree columns. So raising trees per group, or a dataset with many groups, shifts what one grid point means. Under LORO every router's tree block grows: PM2.5 +18%, fire +30%, defo +64%, NDVI +48%. The 1000x grid absorbs most of it, but edge picks are more likely; they are flagged, never extended.
- **Per-group noise (LORO).** With 15 scored units per group on PM2.5 and NDVI (720 test rows), one group's score is noisy. Per-group tables print the unit count and a noise note. Decisions use the pooled headline and the jackknife over all groups, never a single group.
- **Spatial neighbours are always among the donors (LORO).** Under the old split, a PM2.5 test region's nearest donor was a median 777 km away centre to centre, or about 337 km edge to edge (CLAIMS_LEDGER.md PM-10). Now every region's neighbours lend trees. Coral's Part 533 ablation shows how much this can matter: removing donors within 500 km took its R2 from +0.20 to -0.56. So the report prints each group's nearest-donor distance and scores by distance band, with the band edges at the quartiles of the per-unit distances (no hand-set edge); a 0 km distance, i.e. a shared or touching unit, is flagged and points to the overlap block (R14). The paper's "unseen regions" wording becomes "held-out region, with its neighbours among the donors".
- **Overlapping groups (LORO).** 8 pairs of regions share cells on the panel (brigalow_belt holds 8,251 of australian_mulga's cells). Dropping trees by group label alone would let a router use trees trained on its own pixels. The overlap-block rule (R14) closes this, for routers, Type-3 services, rival folds and the blocked jackknife. It is derived from the static table, so a new package gets it without a hand-set list. The continuity row inherits the overlaps the old splits already had.
- **The group jackknife may be too narrow (LORO).** Every group is scored, and neighbouring groups' test-window errors can move together. The ruled jackknife treats them as independent. The blocked jackknife is printed beside, report only, with a flag when its SE is more than 20% above the group SE. Near-but-not-touching groups are blocked by neither; this is disclosed.
- **Rivals' fold disclosure (LORO).** Each donor-only rival model sees about 90% of the groups, while each engine router sees the trees of every group outside its own block. The gauged global rival also sees the scored unit's own group up to the cut. Hyperparameters are tuned once on groups a fold model later never saw, and Board B follows the owner's choice (9.1 item 6c). All are printed with every rival table (4.6).
- **Comparability with old numbers (LORO).** The continuity row is not a replication. Its routers see more trees, fewer units per group are scored, and the band comes from all groups. Old published numbers are quoted as "previous design" and never differenced against LORO numbers as if they came from one experiment.
- **Defo's cost under LORO.** 42 pooled region solves of 4,101 columns take about 306 min, and its tables are rebuilt per group. The bank-time line (A5) is the least certain part of the cost model. The optional shared Gram (9.1 item 6d) removes about 3 h.
- **Convention changes move fire's and defo's numbers** beyond the ruled changes (3.3). They are disclosed as their own rows in the pre-registration.
- **Per-problem risk moves into dataset scripts** (fire's key map, NDVI's 435-feature rebuild, defo's matrix). They sit outside the engine hash but inside the package fingerprint, and the owner reviews them as science.
- **The harness proves moved components, not the new headline path.** The regression gates cannot hold on headline settings, because the rules change the numbers. G-LEGACY keeps the harness from becoming a back door.
- **The shipped thread state is unknown** (numpy 4 threads, scipy 16), so pinning may itself move numbers. The goldens are captured under the pin.
- **Declarations are enforced, not detected.** A wrong window, composite date or lineage fact passes the checker, so the planted-fault table says 'rule enforcement'.
- **FitContext, the neighbour inner-cut bank, the own-block column mask, the masked Type-3 services and the as-of view are a structural refactor.** A bug there could create the very leak it targets. The defence is owner-signed validators, planted faults and fitctx_log audits.
- **Disk:** NDVI's tables are about 26.7 GB per kind under LORO (defo's about 56 GB), against about 52 GB free. Per-group batching is proven in M2; temp matrices are always deleted. The machine can refuse a run but never shrink one.
- **RAM:** 15.8 GB; PM2.5 once lost about 2 h to paging. One heavy job at a time; no GUI, browser or games during runs.
- **MAE selection on zero-heavy targets can pick timid arms.** predict-zero, corr2, detection and the calibration ratio are printed beside every MAE.
- **Type-3 gates that rank across the whole test window** are look-ahead by construction. They are stamped, or rewritten per issue month by the dataset.
- **Coral under the uniform convention was thin** (452 test rows in 24 ecoregions under the old split). Under LORO every ecoregion is tested, so most of that thinness goes; the scored count is re-derived at M6b. It stays optional; the owner decides at M6b.
- **Calendar-year windows** need about 7 years of target data even with the short-record ladder (R5), and they split seasons that cross Dec/Jan. Shorter records cannot run. This is stated in the refusals, and no per-dataset window option is added.
- **Defo's shipped banks cannot be rebuilt** (defo_corrector.py:173-178), so the new banks differ for reasons beyond the rule changes. This is disclosed.
- **Overpromising.** 'Plug and predict' means no modelling options, not no work. The human data audit dominates, and the clickable GUI arrives after submission.
- **Python 3.9 is past end of life,** and oneDAL picks code paths per CPU. Reviewers' tier-3 runs need tolerances and an exact lock file.
